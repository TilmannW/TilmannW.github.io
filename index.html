About

Heidelberg University

I am a PhD candidate in Political Economy at Heidelberg University, supervised by Prof. Axel Dreher.

In my research, I study questions in political economy and development economics. My current work examines political communication and public opinion, distributive politics, migration, international development finance, and the political consequences of economic reforms.

My research combines large-scale survey, administrative, geospatial, and digital-trace data with applied microeconometric methods. Current projects study political talk shows and party support in Germany, ethnic mobilization and aid allocation in Sub-Saharan Africa, Chinese mobility to Africa, electoral cycles in migration policy, and IMF-supported reforms.

Before joining Heidelberg University, I studied International Political Economy at Philipps-Universität Marburg and the University of Texas at Dallas, International and Development Economics at HTW Berlin, and Socioeconomics at the University of Hamburg.
