I am a PhD candidate in Political Economy at Heidelberg University, supervised by Prof. Axel Dreher.

In my research, I study questions in political economy and development economics. I am particularly interested in political communication and public opinion, distributive politics, migration, international development finance, and the political consequences of economic reforms.

My current work examines how political talk shows affect party support and economic expectations in Germany, how ethnic mobilization shapes the allocation of development resources in Sub-Saharan Africa, and how Chinese-financed development projects create temporary mobility links between China and Africa.

I also work on electoral cycles in migration policy, IMF-supported economic reforms, and the long-run relationship between early agricultural development and cultural evolution.

Before starting my PhD, I studied International Political Economy at Philipps-Universität Marburg and the University of Texas at Dallas, International and Development Economics at HTW Berlin, and Socioeconomics at the University of Hamburg.
