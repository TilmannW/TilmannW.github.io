Talk Shows, Populism, and the Far Right: Evidence from Germany's Political Talk-Show Universe, 1998–2025

Tilmann Waffenschmidt

Work in progress

Draft available upon request

Abstract
Broadcast media firms influence political competition by deciding which actors gain access to prominent public platforms. This project studies how appearances by different parties on German political talk shows shape party support and economic expectations, with particular attention to populist parties and the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD). It combines the Talkshow Dataset of Germany, covering the political talk-show universe from 1998 to 2025, with more than 3.3 million daily forsa survey responses.

The Loyalty Paradox: Ethnic Mobilization, Public Resources, and Electoral Strategy in Sub-Saharan Africa

with Cäcilia Stapper

Work in progress

Draft available upon request

Abstract
Do governments reward ethnic loyalty with public resources, or do they use them to increase future support when ethnic mobilization declines? We introduce a time-varying measure of ethnic mobilization and combine geocoded Afrobarometer data with development-aid projects to study how electoral support and the allocation of politically steerable resources move together across electoral terms.

Pressure at Home, Presence Abroad: Development Projects and Chinese Mobility to Africa

with Tobias Heidland, Annika Kaiser, Linda Lochner, and Song Yuan

Work in progress

Draft available upon request

Abstract
Chinese-financed development projects shape more than the allocation of capital across Africa; they also serve as temporary channels for cross-border mobility. We construct mobility links between Chinese prefectures and African destinations from geolocated Weibo posts and combine them with geocoded development projects and local economic shocks in China.

Ballots, Borders, and Election Cycles: How Elections Influence Migration Politics

Tilmann Waffenschmidt

Work in progress

Abstract
This project studies how elections influence migration politics. It combines cross-national data on asylum decisions with evidence on the allocation of refugees across German municipalities to examine whether electoral timing and political alignment shape migration-policy implementation.

Globalization and Discontent: How Globalization Fuels Political Polarization

with Valentin Lang

Work in progress

Abstract
This project examines whether economic reforms promoted through IMF lending programs contribute to political polarization and the rise of populist parties. It studies how IMF conditionality affects reform dynamics and traces the political consequences of the resulting economic changes.

Seeds of Change: Dietary Composition and Cultural Evolution in Early Human Societies

with Tobias Korn

Work in progress

Abstract
This project studies the relationship between the composition of Neolithic diets and long-run sociocultural development using archaeobotanical evidence and data on later values, norms, and institutions.
