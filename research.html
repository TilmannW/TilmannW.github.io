Talk Shows, Populism, and the Far Right

Tilmann Waffenschmidt

Working paper, September 2026

PDF

Abstract
Broadcast media firms influence political competition by deciding which actors gain access to prominent public platforms. This paper studies how appearances by different parties on political talk shows shape party support and economic expectations, with particular attention to populist parties and the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD). It combines the newly constructed Talkshow Dataset of Germany (TDOG), covering more than 6,000 political talk-show episodes from 1998 to 2025, with more than 3.3 million daily forsa survey responses. The results provide high-frequency evidence that political visibility can generate immediate and heterogeneous shifts in public opinion while media gatekeeping decouples platform access from electoral strength.

The Loyalty Paradox: Ethnic Mobilization, Public Resources, and Electoral Strategy in Sub-Saharan Africa

with Cäcilia Stapper

Working paper, July 2026

PDF

Abstract
Do governments reward ethnic loyalty with public resources, or do they use them to increase future support when ethnic mobilization declines? We introduce a new measure of ethnic mobilization and combine Afrobarometer data with geocoded aid projects. Contrary to a reward logic, declines in ethnic mobilization are associated with increases in bilateral aid projects and flows.

Pressure at Home, Presence Abroad: Development Projects and Chinese Mobility to Africa

with Tobias Heidland, Annika Kaiser, Linda Lochner, and Song Yuan

Working paper, September 2026

PDF

Abstract
Chinese-financed development projects shape more than the allocation of capital across Africa; they also serve as temporary channels for cross-border mobility. We construct mobility links between Chinese prefectures and African destinations from 76 million Weibo posts and combine them with geocoded development projects and construction strikes in China. The patterns suggest that ongoing development projects temporarily connect particular African locations to changing economic conditions in China.

with Tobias Korn

Work in progress

Abstract
