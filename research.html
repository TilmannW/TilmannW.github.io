Talk Shows, Populism, and the Far Right

Tilmann Waffenschmidt

Working paper, September 2026

PDF

Abstract
Broadcast media firms influence political competition by deciding which actors gain access to prominent public platforms. This paper studies how appearances by different parties on political talk shows shape party support and economic expectations, with particular attention to populist parties and the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD). It combines the newly constructed Talkshow Dataset of Germany (TDOG), covering more than 6,000 political talk-show episodes from 1998 to 2025, with more than 3.3 million daily forsa survey responses, comparing respondents interviewed on the broadcast day with respondents interviewed on the following day. Talk-show appearances increase support for the appearing party on average, with the largest party-specific estimate for the AfD. AfD and Left appearances also shift economic expectations in a more pessimistic direction. The results provide high-frequency evidence that political visibility can generate immediate and heterogeneous shifts in public opinion while media gatekeeping decouples platform access from electoral strength.

The Loyalty Paradox: Ethnic Mobilization, Public Resources, and Electoral Strategy in Sub-Saharan Africa

with Cäcilia Stapper

Working paper, July 2026

PDF

Abstract
Do governments reward ethnic loyalty with public resources, or do they use them to increase future support when ethnic mobilization declines? We introduce a new concept and measure of ethnic mobilization, capturing how strongly ethnic groups coordinate electoral support behind the same political party across regions and over time. Combining Afrobarometer data with geocoded aid projects, we examine how ethnic mobilization shapes subnational public resource allocation. Contrary to a reward logic, declines in ethnic mobilization are associated with increases in bilateral aid projects and flows. This pattern is consistent with governments using public resources to win additional future votes among ethnic constituencies whose support is not fully mobilized.

Pressure at Home, Presence Abroad: Development Projects and Chinese Mobility to Africa

with Tobias Heidland, Annika Kaiser, Linda Lochner, and Song Yuan

Working paper, September 2026

PDF

Abstract
Chinese-financed development projects shape more than the allocation of capital across Africa; they also serve as temporary channels for cross-border mobility. We construct mobility links between Chinese prefectures and African destinations from 76 million Weibo posts by more than 90,000 users and combine them with geocoded development projects and construction strikes in China. When labor-market conditions in a prefecture deteriorate, Chinese mobility in Africa shifts toward locations with ongoing Chinese-financed projects, while total mobility from affected prefectures to Africa remains unchanged. The patterns suggest that ongoing development projects temporarily connect particular African locations to changing economic conditions in China.

Ballots, Borders, and Election Cycles: How Elections Influence Migration Decisions

Tilmann Waffenschmidt

Work in progress

Abstract
This project studies whether governments systematically change asylum decision-making around national elections. It combines UNHCR asylum data for democracies over time with a German municipal panel to examine both rejection decisions and the allocation of refugees across municipalities, including whether political alignment between municipalities and state governments matters around elections.

Seeds of Change: Dietary Composition and Cultural Evolution in Early Human Societies

with Tobias Korn

Work in progress

Abstract
This project studies the relationship between the composition of Neolithic diets and long-run sociocultural development. Using archaeobotanical evidence on plant remains, we reconstruct the spatial and temporal diffusion of domesticated plants and relate differences in crop availability and dietary composition to later values, norms, and institutions.

Globalization and Discontent: How Globalization Fuels Political Polarization

with Valentin Lang

Work in progress

Abstract
This project examines whether economic reforms promoted through IMF lending programs contribute to political polarization and the rise of populist parties. It combines the IMF Conditionality Dataset with structural-reform, electoral, and polarization data and uses variation in the assignment of IMF mission chiefs as an instrumental-variable strategy for the composition of reform conditions.
